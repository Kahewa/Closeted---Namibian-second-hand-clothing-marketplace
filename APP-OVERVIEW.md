# Closet Sales Namibia — app overview

A reference brief describing what this app is, how it works, and what
constrains its design. Written to be pasted into an AI as context when
asking for design suggestions.

---

## 1. What it is

A marketplace where Namibians sell their pre-loved clothes and shoes. It is
**not** a general classifieds site — the platform is deliberately scoped to
clothing and footwear only.

The distinctive idea is the **closet sale** (called a "carousel" in the
code): instead of one listing per garment, a seller pays a **flat N$150
fee** and gets one swipeable post that can hold as many items as they
like. Each photo in that post is a separate item with its own category,
size, store/brand, condition and price.

Buyers swipe through a seller's closet, then contact the seller directly on
**WhatsApp**. The platform does not process the sale, hold funds, ship
anything, or take a commission. Its only revenue is the N$150 posting fee.

Money for the fee moves **outside the app** — bank transfer, mobile wallet,
or cash — and the owner approves each post by hand once payment lands.

---

## 2. Who uses it

| Role | What they do |
|---|---|
| **Buyer** (may be signed out) | Browses the feed and shop grid, opens a closet, swipes items, messages the seller on WhatsApp. Needs no account. |
| **Seller** (signed in) | Builds a closet sale, pays N$150 off-platform, waits for approval, then manages it — marks items sold, edits before approval, deletes. |
| **Admin** (exactly one account) | Confirms payments against a reference, approves or rejects posts, sees the live feed, bans/deletes accounts, and posts their own closets free with no approval. |

The admin is a single hard-coded email address, checked both in the client
and in the Firestore security rules. There is no role hierarchy beyond
"admin / everyone else".

## 3. Pages

| File | Purpose |
|---|---|
| `index.html` | Home. Hero + "new drops" feed of full-size closet cards, with a small welcome note beside it on desktop. |
| `shop.html` | Browse everything. A compact grid of closet thumbnails (3 per row); tapping one opens the full swipeable closet in a lightbox. |
| `sell.html` | Build / edit a closet sale. Also handles the payment dialog. |
| `profile.html` | A seller's public closet — avatar, bio, socials, and all their posts. When it is your own, it also shows pending-payment details and account settings. |
| `search.html` | Find people by username. |
| `login.html` | Sign in / sign up. |
| `admin.html` | The owner's dashboard: approvals queue, live feed, account management. |

Navigation is: **home · shop · sell · profile · people**, plus an account
menu. All shared chrome (announcement bar, navbar, footer) is generated by
one module, `js/layout.js`, from a config block at the top of that file.

## 4. Key user journeys

### Selling (the most important flow)
1. Seller opens **Sell**. A dialog explains: N$150 flat, unlimited items in
   one closet, build it now and pay after, it goes live once approved, and
   it stays editable until then.
2. They pick a **username** if they don't have one — this doubles as their
   payment reference.
3. They add items. Each item = one photo + category, size, store/brand,
   condition, price, optional notes. They also give a WhatsApp number.
4. On **Post**, photos upload and the closet saves as `pending`. A second
   dialog then shows where to send the money: a mobile wallet number, full
   bank details, and a WhatsApp link for arranging a cash handover. Their
   `@username` is shown as the reference to use.
5. Those same payment details stay **pinned to the top of their profile**
   until nothing is pending.
6. The admin sees the post in the approvals queue with its payment
   reference, checks their bank, and approves — it appears on the feed
   immediately.

### Buying
Browse feed or shop grid → open a closet → swipe items → tap
"Message seller on WhatsApp", which opens a chat pre-filled with a message
naming the seller and the site.

### Admin
Sign in with the admin email → routed straight to the dashboard instead of
the shopper's feed → three tabs: approvals queue, live feed (can pull posts
back down), accounts (ban, unban, delete, search).

## 5. Data model (Firestore)

Three collections.

**`users/{uid}`** — public profile
```
uid, displayName, email, username, bio, profilePicURL,
socialLinks: { instagram, tiktok, facebook, whatsapp },
banned: bool, deleted: bool (admin tombstone), createdAt
```

**`usernames/{username}`** — one doc per taken name, holding `{ uid }`.
Firestore has no unique constraint; this document *is* the uniqueness
mechanism (rules allow create but never update).

**`carousels/{id}`** — one closet sale
```
sellerId, sellerName, sellerUsername, sellerPhotoURL, sellerWhatsapp,
status: "pending" | "approved" | "rejected",
paymentRef, createdAt,
items: [ { id, imageURL, publicId, category, size, store,
            condition, price, notes, sold } ]
```

Fixed vocabularies (not free text):
- **Categories**: Tops · Bottoms · Dresses & Skirts · Outerwear · Shoes · Other Clothing
- **Conditions**: New with tags · Like new · Good · Fair

Typical shape: most closets hold **2–10 items**; the cap is 30. Prices are
Namibian dollars, usually N$30–N$800.

## 6. Business rules that shape the UI

- A closet is invisible to everyone but its owner and the admin until
  `status == "approved"`. The feed and shop query approved only.
- A seller **can edit a closet until it is approved; after that it is
  locked.** The edit button only renders on non-approved posts.
- A seller can never change `status` — enforced in the security rules, so
  nobody can approve themselves.
- Marking an item **sold** still works after approval; sold items grey out
  and the closet shows "sold out" when every item is gone.
- Banned accounts keep their login but cannot post.
- Usernames are 3–20 chars, lowercase letters/numbers/dots/underscores.
- Deleting your own account removes your posts, username and profile.
  Admin deletion wipes the same but keeps a tombstone so a banned person
  can't sign back in and get a fresh profile.

## 7. Technical constraints (important for design advice)

- **Plain static HTML/CSS/JS. No build step, no framework, no bundler.**
  ES modules loaded directly by the browser; Firebase SDK from a CDN. Any
  design suggestion must be implementable in hand-written CSS — no
  Tailwind, no React, no npm packages.
- **One stylesheet**: `public/css/style.css` (~1,600 lines), driven by CSS
  custom properties. Changing a token cascades everywhere.
- **Class names are coupled to JavaScript.** The JS builds DOM nodes with
  specific class names (`carousel-card__*`, `tile__*`, `btn--primary`,
  `status-pill--*`, etc). Renaming a class breaks behaviour. Some names are
  legacy and read oddly for what they now do.
- **Backend**: Firebase Auth (email/password + Google) and Firestore, both
  called straight from the browser. A small Express server serves the
  static files and provides one endpoint that deletes images. Photos are
  hosted on **Cloudinary** (Firebase Storage requires a paid plan), with
  browser-side resizing to 1600px before upload.
- **No payment gateway.** The fee is manual and confirmed by a human.

### Audience and conditions
- **Namibian market**, mobile-first — assume most traffic is phones on
  mobile data, sometimes slow. Image weight matters.
- Photos are user-taken phone snaps of clothing, portrait-ish, uneven
  quality and lighting. The design has to flatter inconsistent imagery.
- Prices display as `N$150`.

## 8. Current design system

The visual style has been through several rounds. It is currently
**glassmorphism**:

- A cool lavender→blue→violet gradient background that slowly drifts, with
  large blurred colour orbs floating behind the content.
- Every card, panel, modal and menu is frosted glass:
  `rgba(255,255,255,0.15)`, `backdrop-filter: blur(16px)`, a
  `1px rgba(255,255,255,0.28)` border, 16–24px radius, and soft diffused
  shadows. No hard borders or offset shadows.
- White text on the gradient; **pink/magenta `#ff5fa2`** is the single
  accent, used for primary buttons, focus rings, the active tab and
  highlights.
- Fonts: **Fredoka** (rounded, friendly) for headings and buttons,
  **Space Grotesk** for body text. Gradient-filled display headings.
  Uppercase micro-labels at wide letter-spacing.
- A floating glass navbar pill sticky at the top; 1200px max container;
  cards lift and glow on hover; buttons scale on press; motion uses one
  shared easing curve, and everything respects `prefers-reduced-motion`.
- Icons are inline SVG masks tinted by `currentColor` — no icon font, no
  emoji.

Earlier iterations were: a dark neon Y2K look, a soft periwinkle look, and
a pink Y2K "desktop windows" look with hard black outlines. The owner
moved away from all three.

## 9. Open questions a designer could help with

- The feed (full cards) and the shop (thumbnail grid) show the same
  content two ways. Is that the right split, or should one absorb the other?
- Closets have no browsing structure beyond newest-first — no category
  filter (one existed and was removed), no sorting, no favourites.
- Trust signals are thin: no ratings, reviews, or seller history. Buyers
  are asked to message a stranger on WhatsApp.
- The manual payment step is the biggest drop-off risk in the funnel — the
  seller does all the work of building a closet, then has to leave the app,
  transfer money, and wait.
- Sold items stay visible inside a closet rather than disappearing.
- No empty-state guidance for a brand-new seller beyond a single note.
